# MedDefense Health Systems – Board Briefing

**Opening Statement**

MedDefense is not facing an immediate crisis, but it is operating with structural security weaknesses that leave critical clinical systems exposed to the same types of attacks that have recently disrupted hospitals across the country. Our current defenses are strongest at the perimeter and weakest inside the network, where patient care systems, medical devices and administrative systems remain too closely connected.

**Critical Finding**

The single greatest risk is the lack of internal network separation. Today, an attacker who compromises one computer could potentially move to medical devices, patient records or core hospital systems. In a worst-case scenario, this could lead to ransomware spreading across the organization, forcing a return to paper records, delaying patient care and causing significant financial and reputational damage.

**Priority Action 1**

Separate critical systems using internal network segmentation. This limits how far an attacker can move and protects patient care systems even if one device is compromised. Estimated cost: **$35,000**. Timeline: **3–6 months**.

**Priority Action 2**

Deploy centralized security monitoring so suspicious activity is detected immediately rather than after operations are disrupted. Estimated cost: **$25,000**. Timeline: **1–2 months**.

**Priority Action 3**

Implement multi-factor authentication for remote access, administrators and cloud services. This adds a second proof of identity and dramatically reduces account compromise risk. Estimated cost: **$7,000**. Timeline: **Less than 1 month**.

**Business Case**

The recommended program costs approximately **$100,000**, well within the approved **$120,000** budget. This is less than 3% of the **$3.2 million** recovery cost incurred by a comparable hospital after a ransomware attack.

**Closing**

If MedDefense delays these investments, it is accepting a growing risk that a preventable cyber incident becomes a patient care and business continuity crisis.
